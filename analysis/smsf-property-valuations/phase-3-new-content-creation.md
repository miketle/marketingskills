```
## What Is Division 296?
- Treasury Laws Amendment (Better Targeted Superannuation Concessions) Bill 2025
- Additional 15% tax on superannuation earnings for balances exceeding $3 million
- Effective from 1 July 2026
- Now applies only to realised investment earnings (unrealised gains exempted)

## How Division 296 Affects SMSF Property Holders
- The $3M threshold is based on total super balance at year-end
- Total super balance must reflect true market value of all assets
- Property is typically the largest asset class in SMSFs with property
- An inaccurate valuation can push you over or under the threshold

## Why Annual Property Valuations Are Now Critical
- Understated valuations create artificial spikes when corrected
- Overstated valuations may trigger unnecessary tax
- The ATO has already flagged 16,500+ SMSFs with static valuations
- Auditors are tightening standards — "evidence-based documentation from adequately qualified third parties"

## The Risks of Getting It Wrong
- ATO audit and compliance action
- Auditor contravention reports (Reg 8.02B breaches)
- Additional tax liability if values are corrected upward in later years
- Potential penalties for trustees

## How to Prepare Before 30 June 2026
1. Order current market valuations for all SMSF-held properties
2. Ensure valuations are from an independent, qualified provider
3. Include comparable sales data and methodology documentation
4. Get valuations as close to 30 June as possible
5. Discuss with your accountant/auditor before EOFY
```

### Page: `/smsf-valuation-ato-requirements/`

**Title tag:** SMSF Property Valuation ATO Requirements | Complete Guide 2026
**Meta description:** What the ATO requires for SMSF property valuations. Frequency, acceptable methods, auditor expectations, and compliance checklist. Updated for 2026 including Division 296.
**H1:** SMSF Property Valuation: ATO Requirements Explained

**Content Outline (1,500-2,000 words):**

```
## Legal Foundation
- Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 (SIS Act)
- SIS Regulation 8.02B
- ATO Guide to Valuing SMSF Assets
- Link to ATO source: ato.gov.au/...guide-to-valuing-smsf-assets

## What the ATO Requires
- All SMSF assets must be valued at market value for annual financial statements
- Market value = price the asset could reasonably achieve in an arm's length transaction
- Must be supported by objective and reliable data
- Valuations must be current (as at 30 June)

## Acceptable Valuation Methods
- Independent property valuation from a qualified valuer (recommended)
- Online property valuation services (accepted by ATO with conditions)
- Real estate agent appraisal (accepted if it specifies supportable data)
- NOT acceptable: trustee's own estimate without supporting data

## ATO Compliance Crackdown (2024-2026)
- ATO scrutinised 16,500+ SMSFs with static valuations (same value 3+ years)
- Over 1,000 auditors flagged for not lodging contravention reports
- Reg 8.02B breaches now account for 12%+ of all reported breaches
- Message is clear: annual independent valuations are expected

## What Auditors Need
- Evidence-based documentation
- From an adequately qualified third party
- Obtained as close to 30 June as possible
- Including comparable sales or market data
- Clear methodology statement

## Division 296 Implications (NEW for 2026)
- Brief summary linking to the Division 296 page
- Why accurate valuations matter for the $3M threshold

## Common Mistakes That Trigger ATO Attention
1. Reporting the same value year after year
2. Using purchase price as ongoing market value
3. Relying solely on automated valuation models (AVMs)
4. Getting valuations too far from the reporting date
5. Not getting independent valuations for related-party properties

- [ ] Annual independent valuation for each property

- [ ] Comparable sales data included
- [ ] Methodology documented
- [ ] Report from qualified/certified valuer
```
